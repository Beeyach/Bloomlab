## A path is a sequence of decisions

A funnel earns its place by helping a particular person take a useful next step. Bloomwired starts with the customer's situation, the business's capacity and the decision between them. A prospect may notice a problem, compare approaches, choose a provider, enquire, book and buy. Those are different states of awareness. A person searching for an emergency plumber needs a reachable number; someone reading about a future renovation may need a planning guide. Sending both to the same long application adds friction without adding qualification.

## Match the promise to the traffic

Write down the source, the promise made before the click, what the visitor already understands and the next decision. A local search visitor arriving on a specific treatment page should find that treatment, location and next action. A cold social visitor may need an explanation and credible evidence before a booking ask. The offer is the exchange: what the person gets, what they must contribute, who it is for and what happens afterwards. A free consultation is not a guarantee of results. Do not hide a required purchase inside a supposedly free appointment.

Choose one primary CTA at each decision. “Check available times” describes an action; “Transform your life” leaves the action unclear. Remove irrelevant navigation from a focused conversion path, but keep information needed for trust and an informed decision. Proof must support the actual claim: an observed process, permissioned demonstration or accurately contextualised result. Invented testimonials are never proof. Qualification questions earn their place when the answer changes routing, eligibility or preparation; every additional question has a completion cost.

## Measure before prescribing

Cost per lead is acquisition spend divided by attributable leads. Customer acquisition cost uses new customers in the denominator and needs a clearly stated cost scope. Average order value is collected order revenue divided by orders; lifetime value needs a retention horizon and a cost/revenue definition. Do not multiply one month's ticket by an imagined lifetime. If the business cannot produce the denominator, record the metric as unknown and ask for records.

For a fictional campaign, $2,000 buys 200 leads, 100 book, 50 attend and 10 buy $500 each. CPL is $10; the advertising-only CAC is $200. That excludes sales labour and software. A low CPL does not establish profitable growth, and a booking problem is different from a show problem. Examine a comparable period and traffic cohort, locate the loss, then test one plausible intervention. A landing-page rebuild cannot repair a receptionist who never returns enquiries.

## Decide whether to build

Compare a funnel with a simpler intervention: repairing a broken link, clarifying a service page, changing staff follow-up or doing nothing until capacity exists. GHL is appropriate when the operational needs and maintenance ability fit it; a spreadsheet with a responsible owner can beat an elaborate unused account. Custom code needs a concrete gap, a maintainer and a fallback. “I do not know yet; I will inspect the booking records with the owner” is useful judgment. A confident unsupported diagnosis is not.
