# Run an evidence-based account review

## Report decisions, not activity theatre

Retention starts with whether the delivered system still solves the agreed problem. Set a baseline, reporting window, source and denominator. Compare qualified inquiries, bookings, attendance and sales using compatible periods. Separate observed changes from attribution: a better booking rate does not prove your workflow caused every new sale.

For a fictional account, forty qualified inquiries produced ten bookings; the next comparable period had forty inquiries and twelve bookings. Booking conversion moved from twenty-five to thirty percent, a five-percentage-point increase. Without a controlled attribution design, describe the observation and investigate other changes. Do not invent a client testimonial or guaranteed return.

## Define maintenance before the retainer

Create a recurring checklist for broken links, disconnected integrations, consent/DND handling, failed workflows, staff changes, permissions and reporting discrepancies. Distinguish incident severity, response target and resolution dependency. A response promise is not a guaranteed fix time when a third-party service is unavailable.

The retainer should name included checks, reporting cadence, capped change allowance, exclusions, approval process and usage billing. A new location, new integration or redesigned funnel is expansion work unless explicitly included. Price against expected work, risk and support capacity; there is no single correct retainer for every business.

## Earn the next conversation

Maintain an account strategy note: current bottleneck, evidence confidence, next test, decision owner and review date. If the problem is low attendance, proposing more lead generation may simply enlarge the leak. Resolve serious reliability issues before asking for expansion.

Ask permission to request a referral after the customer has acknowledged useful work. Do not fabricate success, imply that support depends on a referral, or treat a referral request as consent to message a third party. Keep the existing relationship and the prospective introduction separate.

The practical computes a supplied comparison and classifies support versus expansion under a fixed contract. It does not grade arbitrary prose, set a universal price or claim a real customer was retained.
