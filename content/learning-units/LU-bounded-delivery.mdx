# Deliver what was agreed, then transfer ownership

A Bloomwired proposal is a decision document. State the client's problem, your recommendation, included scope, price, timeline, assumptions, exclusions and acceptance process. Connect the offer to an observed operating problem, not a generic package of features. Name the deliverables the client can inspect and the outcomes that remain hypotheses. Record who can approve, what constitutes acceptance, when the deposit is due and which inputs start the delivery clock. A friendly email is not automatically approval to expand scope.

## Onboard all nine dependencies

Access means the right account and permission level, checked with its owner. Credentials means an invitation or secure transfer process, never secrets pasted into a learning response. Domains means DNS ownership, approved hostname and a connection check. Calendars means staff, availability, time zone, duration, buffers and location rules. Users means roles, assignment, absence coverage and escalation. Branding means approved assets and permission to use them. Copy means a named writer and approver for pages, forms and messages. Payment means the commercial deposit and an explicitly authorised payment-testing boundary. Expectations means acceptance criteria, response windows, revision limits and the effect of missing inputs. An unanswered item is a blocker or documented unknown, not permission to guess.

## Build around dependencies

For the consultation build, settle data before pipeline: stages and ownership need the agreed contact and opportunity model. Pipeline precedes calendar because booking must move the right deal. Calendar precedes forms because the capture and qualification inputs depend on routing. Forms precede workflows because field mapping and trigger events must be stable. Workflows precede the assembled funnel so the page connects to functioning follow-up. Funnel precedes tracking so the released paths and events are instrumented. Tracking precedes integrated QA, which must test what will actually be released.

Research, copy drafts and small tests can run alongside this sequence. That does not mean a downstream deliverable is ready before its dependencies. When an input slips, describe what completed, what is blocked, the owner and the next decision. Offer a smaller release or revised date where sensible. Do not conceal a blocked calendar behind a polished page screenshot.

## Changes are commercial decisions

Compare a request to the accepted scope and revision allowance. Hold the boundary, exchange deliverables in a written re-scope, or quote the added work and time. Adding a second location affects routing, staff permissions, copy, testing and support; it is not merely duplicating a page. Do not begin expanded work before approval. A correct decision may lose a deal when the proposed budget cannot support a safe delivery. Accepting unpaid scope creep while promising the original deadline creates obligations the build cannot satisfy.

## QA and acceptance

Run the nineteen-area protocol against the release candidate. Keep expected and actual results, evidence and unresolved items. A failed or blocked test requires a hold or an explicitly approved reduction in release scope followed by retesting. Send the acceptance request to the named decision maker with the evidence and open limits. Do not invent client approval. Keep training-environment success separate from a live account acceptance.

## Handoff has four parts

Documentation includes the system map, naming, dependencies, change log, QA record, recovery steps and known limits. Training includes a staff walkthrough and teach-back of ordinary work and failures. Ownership identifies who controls the account, domain, assets, billing and future decisions, and verifies transfer or removal of access. Support defines the window, included fixes, response expectations, escalation and paid change process. Missing any part leaves someone unable to operate or maintain the system. In the handoff practical, describe each part concretely for Priya, Dana and Theo; do not use “training provided” as a substitute for a plan.
