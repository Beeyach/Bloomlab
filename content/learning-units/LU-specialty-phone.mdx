# Trace IVR and phone-routing failures

## A deterministic call tree

Interactive Voice Response uses prompts and keypad choices to route a caller. HighLevel's IVR guide configures this through workflows. It is not synonymous with Voice AI: a fixed “press one for bookings” menu can remain deterministic and may be the simpler system.

Draw the number entry point, menu choices, invalid input, timeout, destination and final fallback. Include opening hours and who owns each destination. A prompt that names a department does not establish that anyone will answer. Test the route through the actual destination before allowing production traffic.

## Failure paths are part of the service

A caller may press an unsupported key, provide no input, disconnect or reach a busy line. Bound menu repetition and provide a useful alternative. Under this training policy, one invalid retry is allowed, then the call routes to a callback capture path; it never loops indefinitely.

Phone numbers, usage charges, recording and consent obligations depend on the business and jurisdiction. Keep these outside a casual template assumption. The authorised owner must verify the applicable policy before a live change. Do not add an unsolicited outbound campaign merely because a phone workflow can initiate one.

## Diagnose from the first missing transition

Use the call record and workflow trace to locate whether the entry trigger, menu, branch or transfer failed. A successful workflow action may not mean a human conversation occurred. Distinguish ringing, answered, voicemail and completed business work when the evidence supports those states.

Rehearse valid routing, invalid input, timeout, closed hours and an unavailable destination. Capture the expected path and observed outcome without exposing a real caller's recording. This local practical repairs the supplied routing table and timeout behavior. No number is purchased, call placed, recording created or live routing changed; the specialty is instruction and deterministic contract work rather than a fake telephone Lab.
