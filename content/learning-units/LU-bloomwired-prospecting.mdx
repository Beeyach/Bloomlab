# Find a problem worth contacting someone about

Bloomwired's useful starting market is a service business whose enquiry-to-booking process has a visible, fixable problem and an owner who can act. A med spa with demand and missed follow-up is different from a new business with no traffic. Your offer should name the customer path you can improve, the bounded system you will deliver, and the evidence you will collect. “More revenue guaranteed” is not a responsible offer. A consultation intake, routing and reminder system is a deliverable; a revenue increase is a hypothesis to measure.

## Research before a recommendation

Read the actual service, geography, pricing cues, booking path, contact options and evidence of active demand. Identify decision makers and whether the team can respond to more leads. Check for a sensible fit between the service economics and your implementation cost. Low volume, no capacity, no owner access or a demand problem can make a technically buildable funnel the wrong project. Record Contact, Maybe or Skip and the evidence behind it. Maybe must name the missing fact and a way to settle it. Skip is a valid professional decision.

Walk the public customer path with permission for any test enquiry. Record the page, time, device and observed result. A broken booking link you reproduced is VERIFIED. A slow test reply is evidence of that reply, not proof that every lead is ignored. A suspected routing problem is LIKELY only when there is a defensible chain of evidence. Internal workflow logic is UNKNOWN without authorised inspection. Never relabel a guess as a finding to make an audit sound impressive.

## A short, useful audit

Separate observation, business implication and recommendation. For Northwind, “the quote form's confirmation gives no response window” is more defensible than “your CRM is losing half your leads.” Explain the likely consequence, then state what you would verify before changing the system. Prioritise one bottleneck using volume, frequency, impact and confidence. A screen full of cosmetic criticism does not establish commercial value.

## Write the first message yourself

A cold email needs an opener grounded in the business, one observed fact, why it matters, one problem and one low-friction next step. Write for the owner, not another builder. For example, ask whether the team wants the short test record before asking for a meeting. Do not insert invented personalisation or claim a relationship you do not have. Bloomwired's positioning is specific systems work supported by evidence, not an agency growth promise.

Social outreach follows the same discipline in less space. Refer to a relevant public post or customer path without turning a personal channel into a long pitch. Stop when the recipient declines. Keep a follow-up record: message, date, response and next action. A follow-up should add a useful observation or answer a likely question. Repeating “checking in” adds no reason to respond. A final close-the-loop message may end the thread without pressure.

## Test your judgment

Before contacting the three businesses in the practical, write the fact that would change your decision for each. One has a plausible system problem, one needs more information, and one is a poor current fit. In the audit, label every claim before writing its recommendation. In the written response, answer the prospect's actual question without inventing their budget, readiness or permission to proceed.
