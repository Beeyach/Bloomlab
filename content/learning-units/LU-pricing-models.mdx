## The same build, six ways

Marcus wants an application funnel. It is about twenty-two hours of work. Here are six ways to
charge for exactly that, and they are not variations on a theme. Each one moves the risk to a
different person and rewards different behaviour.

## Hourly

You charge for time. Twenty-two hours at your rate, invoiced as you go.

What it rewards: honesty about how long things take. What it punishes: getting faster. The better
you get at this work, the less you earn for the same result, which is the wrong direction for a
career to run in.

Hourly also puts the client in the position of watching the clock. Every question they ask costs
them money, so they stop asking questions, and a build with no questions in it is a build with
wrong assumptions in it.

Hourly is right when the work genuinely cannot be scoped. Ongoing troubleshooting on somebody
else's system, for example, where you have no idea what you will find.

<Callout kind="idea">
  If you can write down what "done" looks like, you can price it. Hourly is what you use when you
  cannot.
</Callout>

## Fixed price

You name one number for a stated scope, and you carry the risk of it taking longer.

This is what most good work gets sold as, and it is the one that needs the most discipline,
because the number only holds if the scope holds. A fixed price with a vague scope is a promise
to work for free at some point.

What it rewards: getting faster, working out reusable patterns, saying no to additions. What it
punishes: sloppy scoping. Both of those are the right incentives.

## Project fee

A project fee is a fixed price for a whole named thing, with a start, a delivery and an end. "The
application funnel, live, with your questions moved across, in three weeks."

The difference between "fixed price" and "project fee" is mostly about what the client is buying.
A fixed price can be a fixed price for anything. A project fee is a fixed price for a result they
can describe to their business partner in one sentence. That sentence is what they say yes to.

## Setup fee

A setup fee is what you charge once, at the start, for the work that only happens once. Building
the thing, moving the data, connecting the accounts, training the people.

You will see it separated out most often when there is a monthly fee alongside it. It exists so
the monthly fee is not being asked to pay for the build. A $200 a month plan does not fund forty
hours of setup, and pretending otherwise is how people end up with a year of work already spent
before the client has paid for any of it.

## Recurring

A recurring fee is a fixed amount every month for something that continues. Monitoring, small
changes, being the person who answers when something breaks.

The test for whether a recurring fee is honest: name what they get every single month. If you
cannot name it, they will cancel in month four when nothing visible has happened, and they will
be right to.

## Retainer

A retainer is a recurring fee that buys access rather than a deliverable. A block of hours, or a
standing commitment that you will be available.

A retainer works when the client has a genuine flow of small work. It fails when they do not,
because then they are paying for hours they do not use, and every month they notice. If you sell
a retainer to somebody with no ongoing work, you are selling them a subscription to guilt.

<Depth title="Why unused retainer hours are a problem for you too">
  It looks like free money. It is not. Unused hours accumulate as a debt in the client's head, and
  the month they finally have a big request, they will expect all of it done at once and included.
  Either you refuse and they feel cheated, or you agree and eat a month of work. Retainers with
  rollover need a cap, and retainers without rollover need to be honest about it in writing.
</Depth>

## How to choose

Three questions get you most of the way:

1. **Can you write down what done looks like?** If yes, fixed or project. If no, hourly.
2. **Does anything continue after handover?** If yes, a recurring fee or a retainer, priced
   separately from the build.
3. **Who has more information about how long this takes?** If it is you, you carry the risk, and
   a fixed price is fair. If it is genuinely nobody, hourly is fair.

Most funnel and GoHighLevel work lands in the same place: a project fee for the build, a setup
fee folded into it or named separately, and a modest recurring fee for keeping it alive. What
varies is the number, and that is the next unit.
