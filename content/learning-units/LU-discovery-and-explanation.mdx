# Diagnose together before you pitch

A discovery conversation should leave both people with a more accurate picture of the problem. Getting through a script is not the objective. Start a cold call with identity, a relevant reason and permission to continue. Respect a refusal. A short permission-based opening is more useful than an uninterrupted account of your services. If the person is not responsible for the process, ask for the appropriate next step rather than pressing for a sale.

## Set a shared agenda

For a scheduled discovery or Zoom-style call, confirm time, the desired outcome and who needs to be involved. Ask about the current process in the client's order: where enquiries arrive, who responds, how someone books, how staff know what happens next and what happens when an appointment changes. Ask for a recent example. Reflect the answer before moving to a new subject. Speaking for most of the call makes it difficult to discover anything.

Establish volume, pain, impact and urgency. Ten missed replies per day creates a different operating problem from one per month. Ask how the client knows the number. Record uncertainty when a figure is an estimate. Clarify existing tools, staff roles, stakeholders, budget and the decision process. Do not demand a budget as a substitute for diagnosing scope. Ask what would make a change worthwhile and what would make it unacceptable.

## Technical discovery without interrogation

Determine account access, ownership, data sources, duplicate identity rules, calendars, location routing, communication permission, exception handling and maintenance responsibility. Translate the reason for each question: “Who should see the enquiry if Dana is away?” elicits a better operational answer than asking about assignment actions. Do not promise an integration you have not checked. “I do not know yet; I will verify the supported connection and failure behaviour before we scope it” is a useful answer.

Summarise the current process, consequence and desired result, then ask the client to correct it. A pitch before this agreement can solve the wrong problem. Explain the smallest credible system that addresses the agreed bottleneck, its dependencies and its limits. In a screen-shared presentation, show the customer path before the workflow. Use the client's numbers only with their assumptions attached.

## One system, two explanations

An owner needs to know who receives the enquiry, what the customer sees, how a booking changes the next action and how staff recover when communication fails. The builder needs trigger, conditions, data ownership, timing, re-entry, exit and recovery rules. Both versions must describe the same implemented behaviour. Do not promise that a reminder prevents every no-show. Explain what the system can do and what remains a staff responsibility.

Use the frame problem, consequence, system and intended outcome. End by checking understanding and agreeing an explicit next step with an owner and date. Closing may mean a proposal, further investigation, a later decision or a respectful no. When someone delays or ghosts, ask for the decision boundary without manufacturing urgency. In the practical calls, listen for the question being asked, diagnose before pitching and keep unsupported claims out of the recap. AI Off still permits the authored conversation; automated speech quality is not a substitute for later human acceptance.
