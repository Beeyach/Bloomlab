# Read prospecting and reporting without invented certainty

## A prospect report is a starting point

HighLevel's Prospecting Tool finds local businesses and produces marketing audit information. A report can suggest an issue worth checking; it does not reveal the owner's revenue, internal workflow failures or willingness to buy. Keep findings labelled observed, likely or unknown and verify the underlying page before making a claim.

Do not treat a public business listing as permission for unlimited outreach. Scope the next investigation, record the source and use the existing evidence-based sales discipline. This lesson does not scrape a site, add a prospect to a real CRM or initiate contact.

## Reconcile reporting definitions

Ad reporting surfaces metrics from connected advertising accounts. Spend, clicks, platform conversions and closed-won revenue are different measures. A platform's attributed conversion is not automatically a deduplicated customer or cash receipt. Check currency, attribution window, date range, timezone and event definition before combining reports.

HighLevel Custom Reports can use configured widgets and schedules. The current guide notes that ad-source timezone behavior can differ from the sub-account's reporting date-range timezone. A beautiful combined report with mismatched windows can be mathematically precise and still misleading.

## Make the next decision auditable

State the business question first, then choose a denominator and source. If fifty ad-platform conversions reconcile to forty unique CRM leads, investigate identity and event definitions before summing them to ninety. Annotate missing integrations and delayed data rather than filling the gap with invented zeros.

A useful advanced report recommends the smallest next check and identifies its owner. The practical reconciles duplicate measurements and scopes an audit claim under supplied facts. It creates no custom live dashboard, connects no advertising account and makes no causal ROI claim. These are full-graph specialties with instruction and local analysis; the existing Reporting Lab remains a narrower shared-account simulator.
