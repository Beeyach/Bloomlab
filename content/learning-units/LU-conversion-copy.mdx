## Keep the promise made before the click

Bloomwired's page review starts with the ad or link that brought the visitor. Carry the same offer, audience and location into the headline and opening explanation. A page advertising a free consultation cannot quietly become a membership checkout. Show what the person gets, who it suits, the important limits and what happens after the CTA. The words should let the visitor decide, not merely sound impressive.

## Build a hierarchy around questions

The headline identifies the offer. The opening explains its relevance. The outcome section makes a bounded promise. Proof supports that promise without inventing client results. The CTA names the next action and sits where the visitor has enough context to act. A form asks for information that the next operation uses. This can fit on one step or several; there is no universal block order beyond the dependencies the brief establishes. A survey before booking is useful only when its answers actually change qualification or preparation.

Write the copy yourself. Prefer an observable statement to a guarantee: a consultation can explain options and next steps; it cannot guarantee a treatment outcome. Replace unexplained terminology with language the owner and visitor use. Check proof at the level of the claim: a demonstration of a booking workflow establishes how the workflow behaves, not increased client revenue. If no outcome evidence exists, say what the service includes and what will be measured later.

## Make the mobile decision complete

Inspect the page at desktop, tablet and phone widths. Read the offer, limitations, proof, labels and confirmation without relying on hover. Follow every CTA, enter realistic long values, submit invalid data and correct it. Check the keyboard path and whether the visitor can return from a booking error. Removing essential qualification or pricing information on mobile produces a different promise. Reduce layout clutter instead.

## GHL and the training boundary

HighLevel's Funnels and Websites share a page-building system. Funnels usually serve a directed sequence; Websites can support several navigation paths. Native pages use sections, rows, columns and elements. Bloomlab's Funnel Lab models conversion steps, block purpose and connections to forms, surveys, calendars and products; it does not reproduce the native editor, domain connection or publishing controls. Use the registered feature reference for the current boundary, then practise the native page manually in GHL.

Payments basics means understanding the transaction the CTA creates. State whether payment is one-time or recurring, what is included and when the next charge occurs. A connected product in a training checkout is not a real payment processor. Never add checkout to a free consultation path just because a product exists in the account. More advanced subscription and failure handling belongs to later curriculum.
