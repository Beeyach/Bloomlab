## The four numbers

Every service business you will ever help has the same shape, whatever it sells. People show
up, some of them book, some of those arrive, and some of those buy. Four stages, four rates:

- **Booking rate** — leads who book a consultation.
- **Show rate** — booked consultations that happen.
- **Close rate** — consultations that turn into a sale.
- **Average ticket** — what a sale is worth.

Glowhaus Med Spa gets about 140 leads a month. Roughly 46% book, 58% of those show, and 35% of
those buy a treatment plan worth around $410. Multiply through and you get 13 sales and about
$5,300 a month from ads that cost $2,400.

<Callout kind="idea">
  The owner does not experience percentages. Priya experiences "the phone rings and then nothing
  happens". Your job is to translate in both directions.
</Callout>

## Where the money leaks

Change one rate at a time and watch the revenue. Lifting the booking rate from 46% to 60% adds
about four sales a month. Lifting the show rate from 58% to 80% adds about five. Both are worth
doing, but one of them is a follow-up problem and one is a reminder problem, and they need
different systems.

<Depth title="Why show rate is usually the cheapest fix">
  A missed consultation has already cost the ad spend, the booking effort and a slot in the diary.
  Reminders and a same-day rebooking path recover people who already said yes once. That is cheaper
  than persuading a stranger.
</Depth>

## Say it back

Before you design anything, say the diagnosis in one sentence the owner would repeat to a
friend: "About two in five people who book never show, and nobody follows up — that is where the
month goes." If you cannot say it that plainly, you do not understand it yet.
