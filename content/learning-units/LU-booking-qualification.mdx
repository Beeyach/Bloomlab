## Establish fit before reserving capacity

For Glowhaus, the consultation has a purpose, a duration, a location and an eligible host. Explain these before asking the visitor to book. Use a short form for contact details and a survey when several answers determine fit. Ask only what staff use. Define what an ineligible or uncertain answer does: another route, a human review or an honest decline, never a dead end disguised as a successful booking.

## Availability is an operational rule

Configure the calendar's duration, notice, buffers, weekly hours, staff and locations. A slot visible in the browser does not establish that every staff member can deliver that service. Test the first and last available slots, a too-soon booking, an unavailable host and the location the visitor receives. If round robin is used, inspect the eligible team and the assignment result rather than expecting a particular person from an unexplained order.

## Confirmation and reminders have different jobs

Confirmation says the reservation exists and gives the practical details. A reminder arrives in relation to that reservation and offers a usable route to change it. Test a lead who supplied no phone and someone who cannot receive SMS; decide how staff will see the exception. Avoid sending the same message from a calendar notification and a separate workflow without deliberately checking the combined output.

## Changed plans are ordinary cases

Cancellation releases capacity and should stop the messages attached to the cancelled appointment. Rescheduling must carry the new time and location consistently; an old reminder is worse than no reminder. A late booking must not receive a message pretending the appointment is tomorrow when it starts in an hour. A no-show is different from a cancellation and deserves a different follow-up. Test all four separately in the shared Calendar and Workflow Labs, read the execution history and document the next owner.
