## Three questions

Before you store anything about a contact, ask:

1. **Is it a value or an event?** "Interested in the Signature Facial" is a value — it can change
   and there is exactly one of it. "Attended the open house" is an event — it happened, and it
   can happen alongside other events.
2. **Who changes it?** A form, a workflow, a person at the front desk, or the account owner once
   a year?
3. **What reads it later?** A reminder template, an If/Else branch, a smart list, a report?

## Tags record that something happened

A tag is a label with no value. `booked`, `no-show`, `meta-lead`. Workflows can add and remove
them, triggers can fire on them, smart lists can filter by them. A tag is the wrong tool the
moment you find yourself creating `interest-facial`, `interest-membership`, `interest-laser` —
that is a value pretending to be three events.

## Custom fields hold a value per contact

`treatment_interest: Signature Facial` is one field with one value that a form can set, a
workflow can update, and a template can print. Custom fields exist on contacts and on
opportunities; a deal-specific fact like "quoted price" belongs on the opportunity.

<Feature id="GHL-CRM-CUSTOM-FIELDS" />

<Depth title="When a field should live on the opportunity instead">
  If the same person can have two deals at once — two treatment plans, two properties, two projects
  — then anything that differs between the deals belongs on the opportunity, not on the contact.
  Putting it on the contact means the second deal overwrites the first.
</Depth>

## Custom values are the account's settings

The front-desk phone number, the booking link, the owner's first name. They appear in dozens of
templates and change once a year. Put them in custom values, reference them as merge fields, and
a snapshot of the account into a second location needs one screen of edits instead of forty.
