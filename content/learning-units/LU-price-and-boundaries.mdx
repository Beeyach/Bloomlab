# Choose a price you can explain and a deal you can deliver

Bloomwired prices a defined intervention in a service business's customer path. Start with the current bottleneck and the smallest deliverable that addresses it. A one-location booking system and a two-location system with separate routing are different scopes. Count discovery, architecture, build, testing, copy coordination, project management, handoff and expected revisions. Ongoing support is separate work. Neither an attractive competitor quote nor a client's top-line revenue tells you your delivery cost.

## Separate price components

A fixed implementation price purchases explicit deliverables under stated assumptions. A recurring fee purchases named recurring obligations such as monitoring, reporting or support; it is not justified merely because software runs each month. Separate pass-through platform or communication costs from your fee. State the deposit, balance milestone, revision allowance and exclusions so the buyer can compare the whole commitment. Do not describe a deposit as earned revenue from work not performed.

Estimate effort using the actual complexity and a contingency you can justify. Consider missing access, unapproved copy, data quality, location routing, staff training and launch risk. A price must support competent delivery and a defensible margin, but there is no universal correct number. The pricing desk exposes how your scope and quote behave against the scenario, including what remains uncertain. Explain why you chose that scope, what could move the price and what you will verify before contracting.

## Negotiate the work, not just the number

When Summit compares your proposal with a freelancer's lower quote, first establish whether the offers include the same testing, support, revision and ownership obligations. A useful response acknowledges the comparison and makes the differences inspectable. Ask which business outcome is essential. If the budget is lower, remove a separable deliverable, defer a location or reduce a support obligation while keeping the core system safe. Do not quietly remove QA and call the scope equivalent.

A discount with unchanged obligations comes directly from the capacity available to fulfil them. A cheaper offer can still be a good deal if the scope changes honestly. A lost deal can be the correct decision when the client requires unsafe shortcuts, unsupported promises, inaccessible systems or a price below a sustainable delivery. Walking away respectfully is different from threatening the client into acceptance.

## Record the agreement

After negotiation, write the accepted scope, price components, payment milestones, dates, exclusions and remaining conditions. A conversation is not a signed proposal. Keep approval explicit and do not treat the simulated client's response as a real commercial outcome. In the practical, test more than one defensible quote and explain the tradeoff; choose a negotiation action that matches what you actually wrote. Hidden client economics remain hidden until the existing result treatment permits a reveal.
